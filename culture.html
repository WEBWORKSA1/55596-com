Lucky numbers by culture

Most lucky-number beliefs come from sound: a digit is lucky when its name sounds like a good word. This page collects the readings that matter most for gifts, prices, addresses, phone numbers and dates.

Japan

4 (shi) sounds like death and 9 (ku) like suffering, so both are avoided in hospitals and gifts. Goroawase wordplay turns numbers into words: 39 = thank you, 4649 = yoroshiku.

Korea

4 (sa) shares the death sound; lifts often show 'F' instead of 4. Yet 1004 (cheon-sa) means 'angel', and 8282 (pal-i pal-i) means 'hurry'.

Thailand

5 is 'hâa', so 555 is laughter. 9 (gâo) sounds like 'step forward' and is considered lucky; plate auctions favour 9s.

India and South Asia

108 is sacred in Hindu and Buddhist practice. Many Muslims in the region use 786 as a numeric blessing. Numerology based on birth dates is widely used for names and business launches.

The West

7 is the favourite lucky number; 13 is avoided (many buildings skip floor 13). 666 has a dark biblical association. In Italy, 17 is the unlucky one.

Arabic chat

In Arabizi, digits stand for letters: 3 is ع and 7 is ح, so 7777 reads as laughter.

Why this matters in business

Lucky numbers influence real prices. Property listings change house numbers to attract buyers, plate auctions pay premiums for 8s, and retailers set prices like 88, 168 and 888 while avoiding 4 and 250. If your customers include Chinese, Japanese or Korean speakers, check your phone number, address and price points with the decoder.

Questions

What is the luckiest number in Chinese culture?

8, because 八 (bā) sounds like 发 (fā), to prosper.

Which number should businesses avoid in East Asia?

4, which sounds like 'death' in Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese and Korean. 14 and 44 are worse.

Is 7 lucky everywhere?

No. It's the West's favourite lucky number but has a mixed reputation in Chinese.
