Why movies use 555 phone numbers

The story of the 555 exchange, the 555-0100 to 0199 fiction range, and what happened when films used real numbers.

By 55596 Editorial · Updated October 15, 2026

Pay attention to phone numbers in American films and TV and you'll notice a pattern: 555, again and again. It isn't laziness. It's a long-standing industry convention to protect real people.

The problem

Whenever a real number appears on screen, someone calls it. Comic strips, songs and films have all caused floods of calls to unsuspecting households. One well-known case: a 2003 comedy showed a seven-digit number without an area code, and people across the US who had that number in their own area codes were bombarded with calls.

The solution

In the North American Numbering Plan, 555 was historically used for directory assistance (555-1212) and little else, which made it a safe 'exchange' for fiction. Phone companies encouraged studios to use it, and by the 1960s and 1970s it was standard. Older productions sometimes used the letter-style equivalent, 'KLondike 5', because the 5 key carries J, K and L.

What's actually reserved today

In the 1990s, the numbering administration opened most of the 555 range for real assignment, intended for information services. That idea never really took off. The range formally set aside for fiction today is 555-0100 to 555-0199. Screenwriters who want to be completely safe use those.

Other countries do it too

The UK regulator reserves specific ranges for drama, and Australia and other countries have their own fictional ranges. The idea is universal; only the digits change.

Fun fact: 555 is also laughter

To a Thai viewer, a 555 number on screen reads as 'ha ha ha'. See what 555 means around the world.

Questions

Are all 555 numbers fake?

No. Only 555-0100 to 555-0199 are reserved for fiction; 555-1212 has long been directory assistance.

Can I call a 555 number?

Most are unassigned; a call usually won't connect.
